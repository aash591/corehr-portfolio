Migrant worker requirements: the employer guide

What a Kerala employer owes a worker who has come from another state — registration, allowances, wage parity, and e-Shram — and which law actually governs this today.

🏛️ ISMW Act, 1979 — still operative in Kerala 📅 Last verified: 19 September 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read

Quick answer

What is it?
A set of extra protections for workers recruited in one state to work in another — on top of the ordinary wage, PF, ESI and contract-labour rules that apply to any worker.
Why does it matter?
Kerala has a very large inter-state migrant workforce, particularly in construction and manufacturing. These protections are inspected alongside contract labour compliance, and missing them is a common, avoidable gap.
Who does it apply to?
Establishments and contractors engaging inter-state migrant workers — historically from 5 or more such workers under the 1979 Act, which is still the operative threshold in Kerala today.
What should an employer do first?
Check whether you're recruiting through a licensed contractor for inter-state workers, confirm displacement and journey allowance are actually being paid, and help workers get registered on e-Shram.
Which law governs this in Kerala right now?

Nationally, the Inter-State Migrant Workmen (Regulation of Employment and Conditions of Service) Act, 1979 has been subsumed into the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 (OSH Code), in force since 21 November 2025, with central rules notified 8 May 2026. Under the OSH Code, the threshold for coverage is expected to move to 10 or more inter-state migrant workers, alongside a unified registration system.

Kerala has not yet notified its own state rules under the OSH Code — as of mid-2026 it was one of only two states/UTs, with West Bengal, that hadn't begun the state-rules process. Until that changes, Kerala's labour department continues to apply the 1979 Act's framework, including its 5-worker threshold. Watch for Kerala's OSH Code notification, since the threshold and process will shift once it lands.

Applicability

The 1979 Act applies to any establishment or contractor engaging 5 or more inter-state migrant workmen on any day in the preceding 12 months — workers recruited by or through a contractor in one state, to work in an establishment in another state. Workers who move on their own and are hired locally, without being recruited through a contractor from their home state, generally fall outside this specific framework, though they remain covered by ordinary labour law.

Worker benefits and rights

EntitlementWhat it means
Displacement allowanceA one-time, non-refundable payment at the time of recruitment, equal to 50% of monthly wages (or a small fixed minimum, whichever is higher), in addition to wages.
Journey allowancePayment covering travel from the worker's home to the workplace and back, including wages for the journey period.
Wage parityThe same wage rates, hours of work and other conditions of service as workers doing the same or similar work locally — a migrant worker cannot be paid less purely because they are from out of state.
Written particularsA passbook or documented record of the worker's identity, wages and terms, issued to the worker in a language they understand.

Employer and contractor responsibilities

  • Recruit inter-state migrant workers only through a licensed contractor where the Act applies, and confirm that licence is current before recruitment begins.
  • Pay displacement allowance and journey allowance as due, and ensure wage parity with local workers doing comparable work.
  • Maintain a register of the inter-state migrant workers engaged, including their home address, and make it available for inspection.
  • Notify the appropriate government authority of the recruitment and deployment of inter-state migrant workers, in the manner currently prescribed by the Kerala labour department.
  • Support workers in registering on the e-Shram portal, even though registration itself is on a self-declaration basis by the worker.

Registration

Contractor: a contractor engaging 5 or more inter-state migrant workers must hold a licence from the Licensing Officer, similar in structure to the contract labour licensing process, before recruiting or deploying such workers.

Principal establishment: the establishment receiving the workers should be registered where the underlying contract-labour or factory registration thresholds are met, since inter-state migrant worker compliance is checked alongside those registrations, not as a fully separate filing.

e-Shram: this is a worker self-registration portal for India's unorganised workforce, not an employer filing. There's no legal requirement for the employer to file it on the worker's behalf, but actively helping workers register is good practice — it gives them a portable record linked to social security schemes that follows them between employers and states.

Non-compliance and penalties

Contravening the 1979 Act's provisions, or a contractor's licence conditions, carries penalties broadly in line with the contract labour framework it sits alongside — imprisonment and fines on conviction, with enhanced penalties for repeat defaults. In practice, the underlying displacement, journey and wage-parity liability owed to the worker is the bigger exposure, since it doesn't disappear just because a penalty was paid.

Once Kerala notifies its OSH Code rules, penalties for these categories of default are expected to rise substantially, in line with the Code's general penalty framework for unspecified contraventions.

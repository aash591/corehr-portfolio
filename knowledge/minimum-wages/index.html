Minimum wages in Kerala: what employers must pay

Kerala sets minimum wages by trade, not one flat number. Here's how the system works and how to find the rate that applies to you.

🏛️ Central Act, state-set rates 📅 Last verified: 19 September 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read

Quick answer

What is it?
The lowest wage an employer can legally pay, set by the Kerala government for each "scheduled employment" — a defined category of trade or industry — under the Minimum Wages Act, 1948.
Why does it matter?
Paying below it is a direct violation, with no discretion. It's also one of the first things checked in a labour inspection.
Who does it apply to?
Every employer in a notified scheduled employment. In Kerala, this covers almost every trade — shops, hotels, factories, IT, construction, and more.
What should an employer do first?
Identify which scheduled employment your business falls under, then check the current rate for that category on the Kerala Labour Commissionerate's notification page.

Applicability

Minimum wages apply per "scheduled employment" — a specific list of trades the government has notified. Kerala's list is long and covers almost every kind of business: shops and commercial establishments, hotels and restaurants, plantations, construction, factories, and dozens of specific trades.

If your business doesn't obviously match a category, it's still very likely covered under a general one like shops and commercial establishments. We can confirm the exact category for you.

Employee benefits and rights

An employee is entitled to be paid at least the notified rate for their category and skill level (unskilled, semi-skilled, skilled, highly skilled). If they're paid less, they can raise a claim with the labour authority, and the employer can be ordered to pay the difference — with a penalty on top.

Employer responsibilities and benefits

Pay at or above the notified rate, track wage revisions as they happen, and keep wage records that show the rate paid matches the category. There's no direct financial benefit to compliance beyond avoiding claims and penalties — but consistent, correct pay is also one of the simplest ways to avoid disputes and staff turnover.

Wage components and revisions

The minimum wage has two parts:

  • Basic rate: revised occasionally through a formal notification, sometimes after years.
  • Variable Dearness Allowance (VDA): revised more often — typically twice a year — linked to the Consumer Price Index, to account for inflation.

Because VDA changes periodically, the number to pay today may be different from six months ago, even without a change in the base rate. We track this for clients rather than publishing a fixed number here that would go stale.

Non-compliance and correction

Underpayment can lead to a claim before the labour authority, an order to pay the shortfall, and a penalty on top — separate from any other labour-law consequence for the same period (like PF or ESI underpayment, if wages were also under-reported there).

Found underpayment? Correct the rate going forward immediately, and work out the back-pay owed. Fixing it before a claim is filed is always the better position.

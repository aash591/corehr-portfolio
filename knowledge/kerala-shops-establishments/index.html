Kerala Shops & Commercial Establishments Act: registration guide

The basic licence almost every Kerala workplace needs — what it is, who needs it, how to register, and what annual renewal actually involves.

🏛️ Kerala state law 📅 Last verified: 19 September 2026 ⏱️ 9 min read

Quick answer

What is it?
The foundational registration under the Kerala Shops and Commercial Establishments Act, 1960, that legally recognises a shop, office, or commercial establishment operating in Kerala and sets baseline conditions on working hours, holidays, and record-keeping.
Why does it matter?
It's usually the first registration a new Kerala business needs, and other registrations and bank/GST processes often ask for it as proof the business legally exists at that address.
Who does it apply to?
Virtually every shop, commercial establishment, or place of business in Kerala — including a one-person office — is expected to register, not just larger employers.
What should an employer do first?
Apply for registration within 60 days of commencing business, in Form B-I to the local Assistant Labour Officer / Labour Commissionerate online system, and mark the annual renewal date so it doesn't lapse.

Applicability

The Act covers shops, offices, IT and service businesses, retail shops, restaurants, and most other places of business in Kerala — regardless of employee count. Purely agricultural operations and some government or exempted institutions fall outside it. Hybrid setups — a home office, a co-working desk, a business with no fixed premises — are the cases worth checking with us individually.

Branches: each place of business is generally expected to be registered/covered in its own right — treat each Kerala location as its own compliance point rather than assuming one certificate covers every branch, and confirm the specific branch procedure with the Labour Commissionerate.

Employee benefits and rights

This Act is primarily a regulatory framework rather than a direct benefit scheme, but it sets baseline working conditions that employees are entitled to at a registered establishment: defined working hours, a weekly holiday, rules on overtime and spread-over of hours, and conditions around employment of young persons and women at certain hours. See our working hours & leave guide for the detail.

Employer responsibilities and benefits

Core obligations: register within the prescribed time, display the certificate and required notices at the establishment, follow the Act's working-hours and holiday rules, maintain prescribed registers, and renew the certificate annually. Beyond the legal requirement, holding a valid, current certificate is frequently asked for by banks, landlords, GST authorities, and corporate clients doing vendor verification — it is treated as basic proof a business is operating legitimately.

Registration

When it becomes necessary: within 60 days of the establishment commencing work — this is not something to defer until "the business is more settled."

Authority: the Kerala Labour Commissionerate, through the local Assistant Labour Officer, with registration filed on the Commissionerate's online portal (Form B-I).

  • Business: proof of business (PAN, GST registration if held, partnership deed/incorporation certificate as applicable).
  • Authorised signatory: Aadhaar and PAN of the proprietor/partner/director, a recent photograph.
  • Premises: lease/rent agreement or ownership proof for the business address, and local body (panchayat/municipality/corporation) licence or tax receipt for the premises.
  • Employees: employee count and basic details, as required by the online form (even a nil-employee establishment must state so).

Government fees: graded by employee count, from ₹70 for a nil-employee establishment up to ₹5,250 for 101 or more employees. This replaces the older table (₹10 to ₹2,000+), following the Kerala Shops and Commercial Establishments (Amendment) Rules, 2025, notified in the Kerala Gazette on 4 February 2026.

Processing time: the Act doesn't set a fixed turnaround. Budget for a few working days once your application and documents are complete.

After registration: display the registration certificate at the premises, and start tracking your annual renewal date from the date of issue.

Renewal and changes

Registration is valid for one year from the date of issue and must be renewed every year, with the renewal application (with the existing certificate and prescribed fee) submitted at least 30 days before expiry. A belated renewal costs an extra 25% on top of the normal fee.

Changes to intimate: change of address, change of ownership/management, change in nature of business, and material changes in employee count (which can shift the applicable fee band at renewal). These are filed as amendments on the same portal, not a fresh registration, in most cases.

Closure: on permanently closing the establishment, the employer should formally surrender/intimate closure to the Labour Commissionerate rather than simply letting the registration lapse — an unrenewed but never-surrendered registration can still create compliance questions later.

Non-compliance and correction

Operating without valid registration risks a fine under the Act, on top of the belated-renewal surcharge above. The Act sets fines per section breached: up to ₹50 for some provisions, up to ₹250 (first offence) or ₹500 (repeat offence) for others. These are the statutory amounts written into the Act itself.

If registration lapsed or was never obtained: apply/renew promptly rather than waiting for an inspection — most Labour Commissionerate processes are built to accept belated applications with the applicable surcharge, and voluntary correction is treated far better than a default found during an inspection.

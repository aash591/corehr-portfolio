Provident Fund & Insurance

EPF, EPS & EDLI: the complete employer guide

What the Employees' Provident Fund, Pension Scheme and Deposit Linked Insurance actually require from a Kerala employer — registration, monthly contributions, the new wage ceiling, and what happens if a payment is missed.

🏛️ Central law — applies across India, including Kerala 📅 Last verified: 19 September 2026 ⏱️ 12 min read

Quick answer

What is it?
A central social security scheme with three parts: EPF (a retirement savings fund), EPS (a pension scheme), and EDLI (life insurance) — all run by the Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO) under the Employees' Provident Funds & Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952.
Why does it matter?
It is mandatory, not optional, once your establishment is covered. Missing it brings interest, damages, and possible prosecution — and it is one of the first things a labour inspector or EPFO officer checks.
Who does it apply to?
Establishments employing 20 or more people (lower thresholds apply to some notified industries), and every employee who is enrolled — including most employees drawing wages up to the statutory ceiling.
What should an employer do first?
Confirm whether you have crossed the headcount threshold, register on the EPFO Unified Portal if you haven't, and check that every eligible employee has an active UAN (Universal Account Number) linked to Aadhaar.
Recent change

The PF wage ceiling just went up: ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 a month, effective 17 September 2026. It's the first change since 2014. The Cabinet approved it, and PIB and the PM's Office confirmed it.

The ceiling rise flows through to EPS and EDLI too, since both are calculated on the same PF wage base. New employees between ₹15,000 and ₹25,000 who weren't covered before are now covered.

Applicability

PF is compulsory for:

  • Any factory with 20 or more workers, in an industry listed in Schedule I of the Act.
  • Any other establishment with 20 or more employees. In practice, this covers most shops, offices, and service businesses.
  • A short list of industries with a lower threshold, such as cinemas. Check with us if your trade is on this list.

Once covered, an establishment stays covered — even if headcount later drops below 20. Smaller establishments can also opt in voluntarily, with majority employee consent.

Inside a covered establishment, an employee joins if their PF wage (basic pay + dearness allowance) is at or below the ceiling. An employee who was already a PF member elsewhere must be enrolled too, regardless of their current wage. This second rule is the one employers miss most often.

Branches: one PAN, one EPF code. Employees across all your Kerala branches (or branches in other states) count together toward the 20-employee threshold.

Employee benefits and rights

SchemeWhat it gives the employee
EPFA retirement corpus built from employee + employer contributions, plus annual interest declared by EPFO. Withdrawable on retirement, or partially for specific needs (medical treatment, home purchase, education, marriage) after minimum service conditions.
EPSA monthly pension after age 58, provided the employee has at least 10 years of eligible service. Also covers family pension on death and, in specific cases, disability pension.
EDLIA lump-sum payout to the nominee if the employee dies while in service. Formula: 35× average monthly PF wages (last 12 months, capped at the ceiling) plus a bonus of 50% of the average PF balance (capped at ₹1,75,000). Minimum payout ₹2,50,000. No premium is deducted from the employee.

To claim, an employee (or nominee/legal heir) applies through the EPFO member portal or via the employer, using Form 19 (PF final settlement), Form 10C (pension withdrawal/scheme certificate), or Form 10D (monthly pension), along with the UAN, KYC documents, and bank details. Common reasons claims get rejected or delayed: KYC not seeded against the UAN, employer not approving the exit date online, or incomplete service history from a previous employer not transferred.

Employer responsibilities and benefits

An employer's core obligations are to register the establishment, enrol every eligible employee, deduct and deposit both shares of contribution every month, file the monthly return (ECR), and maintain records for inspection. Beyond the legal requirement, consistent PF compliance is also a practical business advantage:

  • It is routinely checked in due diligence, empanelment, and tender processes.
  • It reduces employee turnover risk — PF and EPS are valued long-term benefits, especially once the wage ceiling covers more of the workforce.
  • It avoids the compounding cost of interest and damages that comes with catching up on missed months later.

Registration

Registration becomes necessary as soon as the 20-employee threshold (or the applicable lower threshold) is crossed — employers are required to register within a prescribed period of becoming liable, not to wait for an inspection.

Authority and portal: Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO), registered online through the Unified Shram Suvidha Portal / EPFO Employer e-Sewa portal. There is no offline registration route.

Documents, grouped:

  • Business: PAN of the establishment, Certificate of Incorporation / partnership deed / registration certificate as applicable, GST registration (if held), first sale invoice or proof of commencement of business.
  • Authorised signatory: PAN and Aadhaar of the proprietor/partners/directors, a Class-3 Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) or Aadhaar e-Sign for the person signing the application.
  • Premises: Address proof of the establishment (utility bill, lease/rent agreement, or Shops & Establishments certificate).
  • Employees: Name, date of joining, date of birth, and bank account/IFSC details for employees to be enrolled from the effective date.

Government fees: none. EPFO doesn't charge a registration fee. Your ongoing cost is the monthly contribution and admin charge, not a one-time fee.

Processing time: EPFO hasn't published a fixed turnaround. In practice, a clean application is usually approved within a few working days. Mismatches with your PAN, GST, or Aadhaar records are the most common cause of delay.

After registration: obtain the EPF establishment code, generate UAN for every eligible employee (or link Aadhaar to an existing UAN), complete employee KYC, and start filing the monthly Electronic Challan-cum-Return (ECR).

Renewal and changes

EPF registration does not expire and does not need periodic renewal — once registered, the establishment remains covered and the obligation is ongoing monthly compliance rather than a renewal cycle.

What does need updating on the EPFO portal: change of registered address, change in ownership/management structure, addition of branches under the same code, and any change in authorised signatory. These are filed as amendments through the employer portal, not a fresh registration.

Closure: when an establishment permanently closes or stops employing anyone, the employer should intimate EPFO and ensure final contributions are deposited and employees' accounts are settled or transferred — the establishment is not simply abandoned on the portal.

Contributions and calculations

ComponentRatePaid by
EPF (Provident Fund)12% of PF wages
(employer's 12% splits into 3.67% EPF + 8.33% EPS, capped — see note)		Employee: 12%
Employer: 12% (split as above)
EPS (Pension)8.33% of PF wages, from the employer's share, capped at the statutory wage ceilingEmployer (no separate employee deduction)
EDLI (Insurance)0.50% of PF wagesEmployer
EPF Administrative charges0.50% of PF wages, minimum ₹500/month (₹75/month if the establishment has no contributing members)Employer

PF wages means basic pay plus dearness allowance. It's not the same as gross salary. Employers sometimes label allowances separately to shrink this number — EPFO regularly challenges that, so don't rely on it.

Worked example

An employee with PF wages of ₹25,000/month, at the new ceiling:

  • Employee EPF deduction: 12% × ₹25,000 = ₹3,000
  • Employer EPS share: 8.33% × ₹25,000 ≈ ₹2,083
  • Employer EPF share: ₹3,000 − ₹2,083 = ₹917
  • Employer EDLI: 0.50% × ₹25,000 = ₹125

Employees earning below the ceiling are calculated on their actual PF wage, not the ceiling.

Non-compliance and correction

  • Interest (Section 7Q): 12% per year on the delayed amount, from the due date until you pay. This applies on top of damages, not instead of them.
  • Damages (Section 14B): a flat 1% per month of the defaulted amount, since 14 June 2024 (before that, it was a slab of 5–25% a year depending on delay). Damages can never exceed the arrears themselves.
  • Prosecution: repeated or deliberate default can also lead to prosecution, separate from interest and damages.

Found a gap? Fix it before an inspection finds it for you. Register or pay the shortfall, work out interest and damages, and settle through the portal. If EPFO has already issued a show-cause notice, bring in a compliance professional — representation has its own procedure and is easy to get wrong alone.

Kerala Labour Welfare Fund: the complete employer guide

A small, easily-missed contribution — and one that Kerala splits across two different boards depending on whether you run a shop or a factory. Here's how to tell which applies to you.

🏛️ Kerala state law — two separate welfare boards 📅 Last verified: 19 September 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read

Quick answer

What is it?
A small, compulsory joint contribution from employer and employee that funds welfare activities for workers — but Kerala runs it through two different boards: the Kerala Labour Welfare Fund Board (factories and general establishments) and the separate Shops and Commercial Establishments Workers Welfare Fund Board (shops and commercial establishments, constituted under a 2007 government order).
Why does it matter?
The amounts are small, so it's easy to assume it doesn't matter — but it's a recurring statutory obligation checked during labour inspections, and getting the wrong board or wrong schedule is a common, avoidable mistake.
Who does it apply to?
Shops/commercial establishments with 2 or more employees; factories with 1 or more; and other specified categories such as plantations and motor transport undertakings, each potentially under a different one of the two boards.
What should an employer do first?
Work out which of the two boards applies to your establishment type, register with the correct one, and set up the right contribution schedule (monthly vs half-yearly) in payroll.
Two boards — don't mix them up

Shops & Commercial Establishments Workers Welfare Fund Board (constituted under G.O. (M.S.) No. 29/2007) covers shops and commercial establishments registered under the Kerala Shops and Commercial Establishments Act — commonly cited contribution ₹50 each from employer and employee, monthly.

Kerala Labour Welfare Fund Board (under the Kerala Labour Welfare Fund Act, 1975) covers factories and other general establishments — commonly cited contribution ₹45 each from employer and employee, half-yearly (June and December cycles).

Applicability

  • Shops and commercial establishments: liable once 2 or more persons are employed.
  • Factories: liable from 1 employee.
  • Plantations: commonly cited threshold of 10 or more employees. Verification required.
  • Motor transport undertakings: reported as covered regardless of employee count. Verification required.

Employee age band commonly cited for the shops-sector fund is 18–55 years; confirm whether this age restriction applies to your specific employees, and whether it differs for the general KLWF. Verification required for the exact current eligibility age band and any exclusions (e.g. supervisory/managerial staff above a wage or designation threshold, which some LWF schemes exclude).

Employee benefits and rights

Both boards run welfare schemes funded by the contributions — typically including support for education (scholarships for workers' children), medical assistance, marriage assistance, and similar welfare activities, alongside a refund of the accumulated fund (with interest) on retirement or leaving covered employment, subject to each board's specific rules. Verification required for the current, complete scheme list and eligibility conditions of each board, as these are set and revised by the respective board rather than the parent Act itself.

Employer responsibilities and benefits

Core obligations: register with the correct board, deduct the employee's share, add the employer's share, and remit both on schedule; maintain records of contributions. There's no direct financial return to the employer, but consistent compliance avoids penalty exposure and is one of the standard checks in a labour inspection alongside PF, ESI and Shops Act registration.

Registration

Authority: the applicable welfare board (Kerala Labour Welfare Fund Board, or the Shops and Commercial Establishments Workers Welfare Fund Board), each with its own online registration process.

  • Business: establishment registration proof (Shops & Establishments certificate, factory licence, or equivalent depending on category).
  • Authorised signatory: ID proof of the employer/authorised signatory.
  • Employees: employee list with basic details to create individual contribution records.

Registration typically involves creating an establishment ID on the relevant board's portal, then adding employee details before starting contribution filing. Verification required for current government fees (if any) and official processing time, as these were not clearly confirmed in the sources checked.

Renewal and changes

This is not a licence with an expiry date — it is an ongoing registration with a recurring contribution obligation (monthly or half-yearly, depending on the board). Update the registration when the establishment's address, ownership, or category changes; intimate the relevant board on closure and settle outstanding contributions.

Worked example (illustrative — confirm current rate first)

A retail shop with 6 employees, using the commonly-cited shops-sector rate: monthly contribution = 6 × (₹50 employee + ₹50 employer) = ₹600/month to the Shops and Commercial Establishments Workers Welfare Fund. Assumption: all 6 employees fall within the fund's eligible age band and none are excluded by role/designation.

Records to retain: contribution registers/challans for both boards as applicable, and employee lists supporting the headcount used for each period's contribution.

Non-compliance and correction

Delayed or missed contributions can attract interest and/or penalty under the applicable Act and board rules. Verification required for the exact current interest/penalty rate for each board, as this was not clearly confirmed in the sources checked.

If a default is discovered: register with the correct board (double-check you have the right one — shops-sector vs general KLWF is the most common error), and remit the shortfall. Given how small the individual amounts are, most defaults are administrative oversights rather than deliberate — early correction is straightforward and low-risk compared to letting it surface during an inspection.

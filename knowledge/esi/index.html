Provident Fund & Insurance

ESI: the complete employer guide

What Employees' State Insurance requires from a Kerala employer — who is covered, what it costs, how to register, and what happens if a payment is missed.

🏛️ Central law, Kerala-notified coverage 📅 Last verified: 19 September 2026 ⏱️ 11 min read

Quick answer

What is it?
A self-financing social security scheme run by the Employees' State Insurance Corporation (ESIC) that gives medical, sickness, maternity and disablement benefits to employees, funded by employer and employee contributions on wages.
Why does it matter?
It's mandatory once you're covered — not a benefit you can choose to skip — and it replaces the employer's own liability for medical benefit and some compensation claims for covered employees.
Who does it apply to?
Establishments of the notified kind (factories, shops, hotels, restaurants, cinemas, road transport and newspaper establishments in Kerala) employing 10 or more people, covering employees earning up to ₹21,000/month (₹25,000 for employees with disabilities).
What should an employer do first?
Check your headcount and establishment type against the Kerala notification, and if covered, register on the ESIC/Shram Suvidha portal and enrol every eligible employee before the first payroll cycle.

Applicability

The ESI Act, 1948 applies compulsorily to factories, and — through state government notification under Section 1(5) — to other classes of establishments. In Kerala, this extension has covered shops, hotels, restaurants, road motor transport establishments, cinemas (including preview theatres) and newspaper establishments since 1975, with the Scheme reaching full implementation across the entire state of Kerala from 1 February 2017.

  • Employee threshold: 10 or more persons employed on any day in the preceding 12 months, at the notified class of establishment in Kerala.
  • Wage ceiling for coverage: ₹21,000/month (₹25,000/month for an employee with a disability). An employee drawing above this is not covered, even if the establishment itself is.
  • Once covered, always covered: like EPF, an establishment that falls under ESI generally remains covered even if headcount later drops below 10.
  • Contract and agency workers engaged at your premises usually count toward your headcount and may need ESI coverage through the contractor or, if the contractor has no code, through the principal employer.

Some categories sit at the edge — a purely B2B office, an NGO, an educational institution. If your business doesn't clearly match one of the notified classes above, ask us to check before you assume either way.

Employee benefits and rights

BenefitWhat it covers
Medical benefitFull medical treatment for the employee and dependent family members, without a direct cost-sharing cap, through ESIC hospitals/dispensaries or empanelled facilities.
Sickness benefitCash compensation during certified sickness, subject to a minimum contribution history in the relevant contribution period.
Maternity benefitPaid leave for confinement/miscarriage, subject to qualifying contribution conditions.
Disablement benefitPeriodical payment for temporary or permanent disablement arising from employment injury.
Dependants' benefitPeriodical payment to dependants if the employee dies due to an employment injury.
Other benefitsFuneral expenses, and rehabilitation/vocational benefits in specified circumstances.

Claims are made through the ESIC portal or local ESI branch office, using the employee's Insurance Number, with supporting medical certificates issued by an authorised ESI medical officer. Common reasons for rejection or delay: the employee's contribution record has a gap (employer defaulted on payment even though it was deducted), or the required certification wasn't obtained from an ESI-authorised doctor.

Employer responsibilities and benefits

Core obligations: register the establishment and every eligible employee, deduct and deposit contributions monthly, maintain the required registers, and cooperate with inspections. In return, compliant employers get a practical benefit too — ESI-covered employees generally cannot separately claim employer-funded medical costs or certain compensation claims that ESI already covers, which reduces the employer's own exposure for workplace injuries and medical emergencies among covered staff.

Registration

When it becomes necessary: as soon as the establishment is of a notified kind in Kerala and reaches 10 employees.

Authority and portal: Employees' State Insurance Corporation (ESIC), registered online via the Unified Shram Suvidha Portal or ESIC's own employer portal — there is no offline route.

  • Business: PAN of the establishment, registration certificate (incorporation certificate, partnership deed, Shops & Establishments registration, or GST certificate as applicable).
  • Authorised signatory: PAN and Aadhaar of the proprietor/partners/directors.
  • Premises: address proof — a recent utility bill or the lease/rent agreement.
  • Employees: name, date of joining, wages and bank details for every employee to be enrolled from the coverage date.

Fees: none — ESIC doesn't charge for registration. Processing time: your 17-digit employer code is generated immediately once you submit online; there's no waiting period for the code itself.

After registration: generate each employee's Insurance Number (or link their existing one), issue/assist with their e-Pehchan card, and start monthly contribution filing.

Renewal and changes

ESI registration does not expire and does not require periodic renewal — it is an ongoing obligation once granted. Address changes, ownership changes, and branch additions are updated on the portal as amendments, not fresh registrations.

Closure: on permanent closure, the employer should intimate ESIC, clear dues, and ensure the code is not left showing as active with pending compliance.

Contributions and calculations

ContributorRate
Employer3.25% of gross wages
Employee0.75% of gross wages (nil if average daily wage is ₹137 or less — employer still pays their share)

These rates have applied since 1 July 2019. Unlike PF, ESI is calculated on gross wages (not just basic + DA) up to the ₹21,000 ceiling — this is a common point of confusion between the two schemes.

Worked example

An employee with gross monthly wages of ₹18,000:

  • Employee contribution: 0.75% × ₹18,000 = ₹135
  • Employer contribution: 3.25% × ₹18,000 = ₹585
  • Total remitted to ESIC: ₹720

Assumption: the ₹18,000 figure is total gross wages as defined under the ESI Act (which has its own inclusions/exclusions distinct from PF wages), and the employee is not in the ₹137/day exemption band.

Records to retain: the accident register, inspection book, wage and attendance registers, and monthly contribution challans.

Non-compliance and correction

  • Interest: 12% per annum on delayed contributions, from the date of default, under Regulation 31 of the ESI (General) Regulations, 1950.
  • Damages (Regulation 31C): a separate charge, on a rising scale by length of delay — roughly 5% a year under 2 months, 10% for 2–4 months, 15% for 4–6 months, and 25% beyond 6 months. Damages can't exceed the contribution amount due.
  • Prosecution (Section 85): wilful non-payment, delayed payment, or falsification can attract fines and imprisonment up to 2 years, separate from interest and damages.

If you discover a missed registration or payment: register/regularise and pay the shortfall as soon as it's found — self-correction before an inspection is treated far more favourably than a default caught by ESIC. For contested demands or inspection notices already issued, bring in a compliance professional rather than responding without review.

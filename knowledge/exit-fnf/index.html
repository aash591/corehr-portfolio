Resignation, Termination & Exit: the complete full and final settlement guide

What has to happen, legally, when an employee resigns, is terminated, or is retrenched — notice periods, the 2-working-day settlement deadline, gratuity, and the documents every exit needs.

🏛️ Code on Wages, Industrial Relations Code & Kerala Shops Act 📅 Last verified: 19 September 2026 ⏱️ 13 min read

Quick answer

What is it?
The set of legal steps an employer must follow when an employee leaves — checking notice period obligations, paying everything owed through a full and final (F&F) settlement, and handing over exit documents — whatever the reason for leaving.
Why does it matter?
Since the Code on Wages took effect, final wages must reach the employee within 2 working days of their last day — far tighter than the 30–45 day practice many employers were used to. Missing it is now a direct, provable violation.
Who does it apply to?
Every employer, for every kind of exit — resignation, termination, retrenchment, or superannuation. Extra rules apply for employees who legally count as "workmen"/"workers", and for gratuity once an employee crosses the eligibility period.
What should an employer do first?
Build a standard exit checklist covering notice, F&F calculation, gratuity (if due), PF/ESI exit marking, and documentation — so every exit follows the same clock, whether it's a routine resignation or a difficult termination.

Applicability: the four ways employment ends

An employee can leave in a few different ways, and each one follows a different legal path. Getting the category right at the start avoids problems later.

  • Resignation: the employee chooses to leave. Notice is whatever the appointment letter or HR policy says — there's no state-fixed minimum for this in Kerala.
  • Termination for misconduct: the employer ends the employment for a specific proven reason, after a fair inquiry. This is disciplinary, not a business decision.
  • Retrenchment: a no-fault exit because the role or headcount is no longer needed. This is not the same as misconduct termination, and for employees who count as "workmen", it carries its own notice and compensation rules.
  • Superannuation, death, or contract expiry: the employment ends on its own terms — retirement age, death in service, or a fixed-term contract running out.

Whichever category applies, the full and final settlement obligation is the same: pay what's owed, on time, and document the exit properly.

Employee rights on exit

An exiting employee is entitled to a few concrete things, regardless of why they're leaving.

  • Notice or pay in lieu where the employer is ending the employment, under the Kerala Shops and Commercial Establishments Act, 1960, or the applicable retrenchment provisions.
  • Timely settlement of every rupee owed — salary, leave encashment, bonus, and reimbursements — within 2 working days of the last working day, under the Code on Wages, 2019.
  • Gratuity, if they've completed the qualifying period, paid within 30 days under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, with interest if it's late.
  • Exit documents — a relieving letter, an experience/service certificate, and their F&F statement — needed for their next job and for PF/tax purposes.
  • Access to their PF and ESI records so they can claim their provident fund and their ESI coverage is correctly closed.

Employer responsibilities

The employer carries the compliance burden at exit, not the employee. Core duties:

  • Confirm the correct notice period and either honour it or pay in lieu, on both sides — including recovering notice-pay shortfall from the employee if they leave early without serving it, if the contract allows this.
  • Calculate the F&F accurately: pending salary, leave encashment (per the applicable leave rules), pro-rata bonus, any recoverable dues, and gratuity if eligible.
  • Pay the wage components of the F&F within 2 working days of the last working day, and gratuity within 30 days of it becoming payable.
  • Mark the exit date in the EPFO and ESIC employer portals so PF and ESI records close correctly.
  • Issue the relieving letter, experience certificate, and Form 16, and retain the F&F workings for future reference.

The exit and F&F process

  • Acknowledge the exit trigger — resignation letter received, termination decided after due process, or retrenchment/contract-end notified.
  • Confirm the notice period against the contract and, for employer-initiated exits, against the Kerala Shops Act or retrenchment rules.
  • Run exit clearance — assets, systems access, outstanding advances or loans, handover.
  • Calculate the F&F — last drawn salary to last working day, leave encashment, bonus if due, deductions, and gratuity if eligible.
  • Pay the F&F within 2 working days of the last working day (wage components), and gratuity within 30 days.
  • Mark the exit in the EPFO and ESIC portals, and issue any PF/pension withdrawal support the employee needs.
  • Issue documents — relieving letter, experience certificate, F&F statement, Form 16.
  • Retain records — the F&F calculation, notice correspondence, and exit documents, for future reference or dispute.

Notice periods: statutory floor vs contract

Kerala law sets a notice floor for employer-initiated termination. It does not fix a minimum notice for an employee's own resignation — that's a contractual matter, agreed in the appointment letter.

"Workman" (called "worker" in the new Code) is a specific legal category — it generally excludes people employed mainly in a managerial or administrative role, and supervisors drawing wages above ₹18,000 a month. Most shop-floor, clerical, and operational staff qualify; most managers and senior supervisors don't. The extra retrenchment protections apply only to workers who fit this definition.

Retrenchment compensation

A qualifying worker being retrenched is entitled to compensation equal to 15 days' average pay for every completed year of continuous service (or part in excess of 6 months), in addition to notice or pay in lieu. This is separate from, and in addition to, any gratuity due.

What goes into the full and final settlement

ComponentHow it's treated
Pending salaryPro-rata salary up to the last working day, including any pending allowances.
Leave encashmentCash value of unused earned/privilege leave, calculated per the company's leave policy and the applicable Shops Act leave rules.
BonusStatutory bonus for the relevant accounting year, if due and not already paid, under the Payment of Bonus Act, 1965.

Retrenchment compensation15 days' average pay per completed year of service, only for a qualifying retrenchment of a "workman"/"worker".
DeductionsNotice-pay shortfall, outstanding loans/advances, or asset recovery — only what the contract or law allows.
Worked example (illustrative)

An employee resigns with 45 days' unused leave and a last-drawn monthly salary of ₹40,000. Their F&F wage component (pending salary for days worked in the exit month + leave encashment of 45 days at the daily rate) must be paid within 2 working days of their last working day. If they'd also completed 6 years of service, gratuity is calculated and paid separately, within 30 days — it does not have to wait for, or be bundled with, the 2-day wage payment.

Non-compliance and risks

Delayed F&F under the Code on Wages attracts a penalty of up to ₹50,000 for a first offence under Section 54, and repeat defaults are treated more seriously. Delayed gratuity attracts interest for the period of delay, on top of the principal amount.

Termination that doesn't follow the correct process — no notice where required, no inquiry before a misconduct dismissal, or retrenchment without the right compensation — can be challenged before the appellate/labour authority, and can result in reinstatement, back wages, or compensation ordered against the employer. Getting the category (resignation vs termination vs retrenchment) wrong at the start is the single biggest cause of these disputes.

If a delay has already happened: pay the outstanding amount immediately, including any interest due, and document the reason for the delay — a prompt correction is treated very differently from an ignored one if it's ever questioned.

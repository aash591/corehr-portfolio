Wages & Payroll

Payroll, payslips & deductions: what the law requires

Paying salary on time, showing it correctly, and only deducting what's allowed — the basics get checked often and missed easily.

🏛️ Central law — Payment of Wages Act, 1936 📅 Last verified: 19 September 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read

Quick answer

What is it?
The rules under the Payment of Wages Act on when to pay salary, what you can deduct, and what records and payslips you must give employees.
Why does it matter?
Salary payment is the most basic obligation an employer has. Getting it wrong — late payment, an unauthorised deduction, no payslip — is an easy, avoidable violation.
Who does it apply to?
Every employer, for every employee, regardless of headcount.
What should an employer do first?
Check your payslip format shows all required details, and review your deduction list against what's actually permitted.

Applicability

The Payment of Wages Act applies to every employer, with no minimum headcount. Wage-ceiling-linked provisions in the Act mainly affect enforcement mechanics, not whether the core obligations apply.

Employee benefits and rights

An employee is entitled to be paid on time, in full except for permitted deductions, and to receive a payslip for every pay period. If wages are withheld, delayed, or wrongly deducted, the employee can raise a claim with the labour authority.

Employer responsibilities and benefits

Pay wages by the due date, issue a payslip every pay period, only make permitted deductions, and keep wage records available for inspection. Clean payroll records also make audits, funding due diligence, and dispute resolution far easier.

Permissible deductions

Only these categories of deduction are allowed:

  • Statutory deductions — income tax, PF, ESI, Professional Tax, Labour Welfare Fund
  • Fines, only for specified acts of misconduct, and only after following the required procedure
  • Deductions for damage or loss directly attributable to the employee's own default or negligence
  • Deductions for house accommodation or amenities provided by the employer, if accepted by the employee
  • Recovery of advances or loans, within the permitted limits
  • Deductions for absence from duty, proportionate to the period absent
  • Court-ordered or legally authorised deductions

Anything outside this list — an arbitrary penalty, an unexplained "adjustment", a deduction the employee never agreed to — isn't permitted, regardless of what the employment contract says.

Recurring compliance

FrequencyWhat's due
Every pay periodPay wages by the due date and issue a payslip showing gross pay, each deduction, and net pay
OngoingMaintain a wage register and attendance records supporting each payment
On requestProduce wage records for inspection

Wage payment is generally due by the 7th of the following month for smaller establishments, and the 10th for larger ones — confirm which applies to your headcount.

Non-compliance and correction

Delayed payment or an unauthorised deduction can lead to a claim before the labour authority, an order to repay the amount, and a penalty — sometimes several times the amount wrongly withheld.

Found an issue? Refund any wrongly deducted amount and fix the process going forward. This is straightforward to correct if caught early.

Kerala Professional Tax: the complete employer guide

A half-yearly tax collected by your local Panchayat, Municipality or Corporation — not a state tax department. Here's who it applies to, the slab rates, and how to stay on schedule.

🏛️ Local body tax — Kerala Panchayat Raj / Municipality Act 📅 Last verified: 19 September 2026 ⏱️ 8 min read

Quick answer

What is it?
A half-yearly tax on salaries and professional income, levied under the Kerala Panchayat Raj Act and the Kerala Municipality Act, collected not by a state tax department but by the local Grama Panchayat, Municipality, or Municipal Corporation where the establishment or employee is based.
Why does it matter?
It's a statutory deduction from salary the employer is responsible for withholding and remitting — getting it wrong (or not registering at all) is a recurring, easily-noticed compliance gap at the local body level.
Who does it apply to?
Employees whose half-yearly salary is above the local body's minimum threshold, and employers who must register, deduct, and remit on their behalf; self-employed professionals are separately assessed.
What should an employer do first?
Register the establishment with the relevant local body, confirm the applicable slab table (Panchayat vs Municipality vs Corporation rates can differ), and set up payroll to deduct correctly each half-year.

Applicability

Professional Tax applies to salaried employees, and separately to self-employed professionals and traders, above a minimum income set by the collecting local body. Employees earning more than ₹12,000 in a half year are liable, under the Kerala Municipality Amendment Act, 2015. Panchayats, Municipalities and Corporations all use this same threshold, though their slab rates above it differ.

Employer obligation: the employer deducts Professional Tax from eligible employees' salary and remits it to the local body — this is not optional withholding left to the employee.

Multiple locations: because collection is local-body-based, an employer with branches in different Panchayats, Municipalities or Corporations across Kerala may need to register with, and potentially follow a different slab table for, each local body.

Employee benefits and rights

Professional Tax is a tax, not a benefit scheme. The employee's entitlement is simple: correct deduction, no over-deduction, and it should show on their payslip. It's also deductible from their taxable salary income under the Income Tax Act.

Employer responsibilities and benefits

Core obligations: register with the relevant local body/bodies, deduct Professional Tax correctly from each eligible employee every half-year, remit it by the due date, and keep records available for local body verification. There is no direct "business benefit" beyond the basic benefit of every statutory compliance — avoiding penalty, interest, and complications when seeking other local body clearances (trade licence renewal, building/occupancy certificates, etc.), where outstanding PT dues can become a sticking point.

Registration

When it becomes necessary: once the establishment has employees liable to Professional Tax, or the establishment/professional itself is liable as a self-employed assessee.

Authority: the local Grama Panchayat, Municipality, or Municipal Corporation where the establishment is situated — registration and payment are increasingly available through the respective local body's online portal, though processes can still vary by local body.

  • Business: PAN, business registration proof (Shops & Establishments certificate, GST registration, incorporation documents as applicable).
  • Authorised signatory: ID proof of the proprietor/partner/director.
  • Premises: address proof / property tax receipt for the establishment within that local body's jurisdiction.
  • Employees: salary details needed to assess which employees cross the threshold.

Fees: registration itself isn't separately charged. Your recurring cost is the tax itself, each half year.

Renewal and changes

Professional Tax registration does not follow an annual renewal cycle like a licence — it is an ongoing registration with a recurring half-yearly payment obligation. What needs updating with the local body: change of address (which may also mean a change of collecting local body), closure of the establishment, and material changes in employee headcount or salary bands that affect assessment.

Closure: intimate the local body on closure and clear outstanding dues — an unresolved PT account can surface later during other local body clearances for the same premises.

Non-compliance and correction

Late payment costs 1% interest per month on the overdue amount, under the Kerala Panchayat Raj Act / Kerala Municipality Act.

If a default is discovered: register/regularise with the local body and pay the shortfall — a business already dealing with one local body (e.g. for a trade licence renewal) is a good moment to also confirm the PT account is current, since local bodies increasingly cross-check dues across departments before issuing clearances.
